A tour of the golem

golem

The golem's screen is a device, not a web page: it never scrolls. Everything fits on the screen, and you move by tapping — a tab, a tile, a drawer that slides up. This page is the map. The examples use Golem Rising, the last song of the Factory Golem setlist, which every golem ships with.

The five hubs: Live, Designer, Songs, Library, Settings

Five icons at the top of the screen take you to the five hubs. They go from now to stock: what is playing, what you are building, what you play, what you own, how the machine is set up.

  • Live — what is playing, and your pedalboard on the screen. It has three pages: Pedalboard, Rehearsal and Concert. See The live screen.

  • Designer — the sound of the song being played: one tab per musician, the chain of blocks, the scenes. See Presets and scenes.

  • Songs — what you play: Songs, Patches, Presets, Banks & Setlists.

  • Library — what you own: Tones (Amps, Pedals, Cabs, Outboard), Keys, Drums, Samples, Tracks.

  • Settings — the machine: Mode, System, Gear, Engines, Content, Connect, About.

The line between Songs and Library is simple: Songs is what you play (things you made), Library is what you own (things you imported). A recording is material you play again, so it lives in Library ▸ Tracks.

Each hub has tabs, and some tabs have their own. The address follows you: Library ▸ Tones ▸ Amps is always the same page.

The top bar

The bar at the top is always there, and its zones never move.

  • The logo, /golem_: tap it to go back to Live from anywhere. On the Live screen its _ blinks on the beat.

  • The mode badge, next to it, tells which mode the golem is in — here Rehearsal. It is absent in Home mode. Hold it for 2 seconds to leave the mode and go back to Home. In a locked mode (Gig), holding the logo for 2 seconds is the way out too.

  • The five hub icons, then the hub's own zone: the setlist on Live, the preset and its scenes in the Designer, the tabs elsewhere.

A mode can hide hubs: in a locked stage mode you only see what you need on stage. Hidden hubs are gone, not greyed — and there is always a way out: the 2-second hold.

Tap, long press, drawers

Three gestures do almost everything.

  • Tap opens. Tap a block and its drawer opens; tap a song or a preset and it loads.

  • A drawer is a sheet that slides over the page. It keeps what you were looking at underneath; close it and you are back where you were. The block drawer has tabs: Settings (its knobs), Presets (its named settings) and MIDI assign (which footswitch or pedal drives it).
  • Long press assigns. A long press on a block tile opens it straight on MIDI assign: put the block on a footswitch or an expression pedal. There is no hidden menu anywhere else.
