Standards: playing along a grid

golem

A standard is a chord chart the golem plays for you: piano, bass and drums — or a manouche rhythm guitar — on the grid of a tune, while you play the head and solo over it. Not a recording: change the key or the tempo, and the band follows.

Playing along a grid

Every golem ships with three: Rooftop Prowl (F, 180, swing — line 01 of the Factory Golem setlist), Rented Room Blues (a 12-bar blues in A, 96 — line 02) and Crooked Lantern (D minor, 200, manouche). They live in Library ▸ Tracks ▸ Standards. Each card shows the shape of the chart, the key, the feel, the meter, the tempo and the form (AABA), and the presets that use it (⚡).

Tap a card to read its whole chart. More standards come from golem.fm (Send to your golem) or from a song folder; ↻ Sync updates the ones you have.

In a preset, a standard is a Standard block on one footswitch: tap to start and stop, hold to restart from the top. While it plays, the Live screen turns into the chart: the bar that plays lights up, with the chorus count — ■ Stop gives the pedalboard back. The tempo is the preset's (or the song's): the block has none of its own.

Grooves and the band

The Standard block's drawer sets who plays and how:

  • Line-up — Combo (piano · bass · drums), Manouche (rhythm guitar · bass), or Custom;
  • Key — the original, or any of the 24 keys; Feel; Choruses (1 to 16); Count-in;
  • Piano — the size of the chords, the voicing (rootless, shell, drop 2…), how the chord is played (comp, block, arpeggio), hits per bar;
  • Bass — walking, roots, two-feel, root–fifth, pedal, bossa;
  • Guitar — the manouche pompe, a waltz, a ballad, a bossa… Rhythm only: the lead is yours;
  • Drums — the kit (Black Pearl, Red Zeppelin, Blonde Bop) and the groove, with fills at the end of a section or a chorus.

The factory charts: Rooftop Prowl plays piano, bass and drums with brushes; Rented Room Blues bass and drums, leaving room for the guitar and the organ; Crooked Lantern bass and rhythm guitar.

The golem never plays a melody, a lead or lyrics: the tune is yours.
