Songs

golem · golem.fm

A song is the thing your band plays: its title, the rig it needs, its tempo, its parts. A preset is a rig; a setlist is an evening; the song is what stays the same from one evening to the next. Every golem ships with one: Golem Rising, an original written for the factory content.

The song page

Songs ▸ Songs is your repertoire. A card gives the title, the tempo and length, the tuning, whether the song is a cover or an original, and the preset it plays — here ⚡ Ambient Metal. ● stage-ready means the preset is on this golem and the song has an arrangement: you can play it from start to end.

The filters at the top find a song again: Stage-ready and To finish, Covers and Originals. Golem shows the songs stored on this golem, Folders the songs read from a folder of .golem files (Settings ▸ Content ▸ Storage).

Tap a song to open its page. The header says what the song is:

  • its title and artist, Cover or Original, and ◆ Factory for a song that ships with golem;
  • in 1 setlist: how many setlists play it. The setlists are listed at the bottom of the page, with the bank it sits on — Factory Golem · #9; tap one to open it.

Instruments, rig, arrangement

The row under the title is the song's sheet:

  • Preset — the rig this song plays. Golem Rising plays Ambient Metal: two guitars, a bass, a voice and a piano, five scenes. Change it here and the song plays another rig in every setlist.
  • Tempo — the song's own tempo. Empty means the preset's tempo.
  • Tuning, Capo, Note — what the band prepares before the song. The phones read them on their Between songs page.
  • File — what the song's .golem file carries: its stems, a score… card only means the song is this sheet and nothing else yet; its stems and score are made on golem.fm.

The rest of the page is the song's arrangement: its parts end to end, the scene each part switches to, and what starts with the song. It is the heart of the song page — see The arrangement.

Everything on this page is the song. Change the tempo or the arrangement here and every setlist that plays it follows. There is no copy to keep in sync.

Originals and covers

Every song says what it is: Cover or Original. The golem never guesses — a new song asks, and a song without the answer is shown as a draft until it gets one. It matters for what leaves your golem: a cover's audio, tab and lyrics are never shared with anyone.

A song is born where you create it: ＋ New song on the golem (a title, cover or original, its preset, its tempo), or on golem.fm. Either way it keeps the same identity on both sides, and the two stay in step: a change made on one side reaches the other. Two songs with the same title stay two songs.

A factory song is golem's, not your band's: it never goes up to golem.fm.
