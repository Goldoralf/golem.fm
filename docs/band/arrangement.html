The arrangement

golem

The arrangement is the song's map: its parts — intro, verse, chorus, solo — end to end, and the scene each one switches to. Start the song and the golem changes scenes for you at the right bar, starts the drums and the stems with you, and stops them at the end. It makes no sound itself: it plays your scenes in time.

The examples use Golem Rising (Songs ▸ Songs ▸ Golem Rising): thirteen parts, 57 bars, 1:54 at 120 BPM.

Sections and bars

The track lays the parts out in bars, one after the other, numbered from bar 1. Each part shows its name, its length and the letter of its scene: Intro is scene A for 4 bars, Verse 1 scene B for 7, the first Chorus scene D for 8.

  • A hatched part has no scene of its own: the scene before it keeps playing. Golem Rising's one-bar Fills do that — they change nothing, they only take their bar.
  • Add a part from the row below the track (intro, verse, chorus, bridge, solo, outro, or + custom): it lands at the end, 4 bars long; drag it where it belongs.

  • Fit / Bars at the right: the whole song in view, or a fixed width per bar to count them.

Tap a part to open it. You set its name, its length in bars, the scene it switches to (one button per scene of the preset, with its name), its time signature, and the drum groove for that part. Under it, a grid reads what every instrument plays in every part — the scene's sound, not a copy of it: to change the sound, change the scene.

The header of the arrangement adds it up: 13 sec · 1:54 · 57 bars @120 — thirteen sections, 1 minute 54, 57 bars at 120 BPM.

When the song starts: count-in, click

The orange row says what happens when you start the song.

  • count-in — bars counted before the song, in the first part's meter. You always hear it, even when the click stays silent for the song. Golem Rising counts one bar.

  • One box per player that can start with the song: Backing (the stems, from the top), Click (from the first beat to the end), Record (a take that starts and stops with the song), Drums (the drum machine, on the groove of each part), Sample (the sounds placed on the track). Ticked, it starts with the song; unticked, you start it yourself. The line under each box says what would stop it: Golem Rising has no stems linked, and no sample block (not in this preset). Its Drums box is ticked: 7 grooves · one per part.
  • The switch that starts the song — switch 9 on Ambient Metal.

  • next part — bars: the parts follow each other on their own, at the song's tempo; play with the click, the stems or the drums to stay together. foot: each tap on the switch goes to the next part, however long you stay in one.

The switch and next part are marked shared: they belong to the preset, so every song on Ambient Metal uses the same ones. Everything else on the page belongs to this song.

  • on /live — while the song plays, the Live screen shows the Stage board (the parts, now and next, the count-in) or your Stomps.

Automation on at the top is the song's master switch: off, the song keeps its parts but the scenes stay at your feet. If the mode does not run arrangements (Settings ▸ Mode ▸ Arrangement), it says so here.

On the pedalboard, tap the switch: the count-in, then the song. With bars, a second tap stops everything the song started. On the song page, ▶ Play does the same.

Rehearsing a section: cue, loop

In rehearsal you work on one part at a time. The Rehearsal page of the Live screen (the Live screen of the Rehearsal mode) shows the same parts as cards.

  • Tap a part: it is where you start. The big button says it — ▶ FROM CHORUS, the bar and the count-in. Nothing plays until you press it.

  • While the song plays, tap another part to cue it: it starts at the end of the current part, on the bar line. The card says NEXT; tap it again to cancel.
  • ⟲ LOOP on the playing card repeats that part until you choose another or stop looping.
  • ■ STOP takes you back to where the run began, ready to go again.

A cued part arrives with its scene, on the bar line. See The live screen for the whole page.
