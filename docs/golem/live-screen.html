The live screen

golem

The Live screen is what the golem shows while you play. Everything on it can also be done with your feet: the screen is a mirror of your pedalboard, readable from where you stand, and a few big buttons for what the feet do not do.

The examples on this page use Golem Rising, the last song of the Factory Golem setlist that every golem ships with. Load it on your own golem (Songs ▸ Banks & Setlists ▸ Factory Golem, line 09) and you will see the same screen.

Pedalboard, Rehearsal, Concert

The Live screen has three pages. Each one answers a different moment.

Pedalboard: the mirror of your feet

The ten tiles are your ten footswitches, in the same place as on the floor.

  • The bottom row, #1 to #5, is your scenes. A scene is a state of the whole band's sound: which lanes play, what each one sounds like. Golem Rising has five: Halo Clean, Glass Verse, Low Riff, Wall Chorus, Soaring Solo. The scene that plays is lit. Under its name, each instrument in turn tells what it plays in that scene — GTR 2 · Clean Echo — so a guitarist and a singer both find their line.
  • The top row, #6 to #10, is your stomps: blocks you switch on and off during a song, like the pedals of a pedalboard. A stomp says which instruments it acts on (GTR, KEYS, VOX) and shows OFF when it is off. In Golem Rising, #6 is a Shimmer on the guitar and the keys, #7 a Tempo Delay, #8 the singer's Grit.
  • Some switches belong to a service: here #9 starts the song's arrangement (SONG) and #10 the drum machine (PLAYERS). They look different on purpose.
  • The two bars at the bottom are the expression pedals. On this preset, EXP 2 is the volume.

A tap on a scene tile recalls the scene; a tap on a stomp tile switches it — exactly what your foot does.

Rehearsal: start from any section

In rehearsal you rarely play a song from the top. The Rehearsal page lays the song out as sections (Intro, Verse 1, Fill, Chorus…) taken from its arrangement.

  • A tap on a section queues it — it never starts it. People touch the screen while they talk; nothing plays by surprise. The card shows START (stopped) or NEXT (playing).

  • The middle of the screen says what happens now — READY · from Intro before you start, the section that plays once you do.

  • The big button says where you start from: ▶ FROM INTRO, with the bar and the count-in.

  • While the song plays, a queued section starts at the end of the current one, on the bar line. The ⟲ LOOP button on the playing card repeats it until you choose another.
  • ❚❚ PAUSE stops and restarts from the section that was playing; ■ STOP goes back to where the run began.

A song without an arrangement simply plays from the top.

Concert: readable from two metres

On stage you need to read, not to touch. The Concert page shows the current section in very large letters, the next section and how many bars are left before it.

  • At the top, the setlist around you: the previous song, the current one with its time, the next one.

  • A thin map of the song, its sections to scale, with the play head. It is only something to read.

  • The transport: ▶ PLAY, and ■ STOP. There is no pause in concert, and STOP needs a 0.6 s hold: a stray tap never stops a song.

Which page you see

The mode decides: Rehearsal mode opens the Rehearsal page, Gig and Jam the Concert page, Home and Pedalboard the Pedalboard page (Settings ▸ Mode ▸ Live screen changes it). The view button at the left of the toolbar lets you switch for a moment; the next change of mode brings back the mode's page.

The toolbar: Mixer, Tuner, Click, Lock, Rec

The toolbar sits at the top right of the Live screen.

  1. Mixer balances what comes out of the rack: one fader per instrument and per player (backing, sample, drums, looper, click). A trim only attenuates — 0 dB is unity, there is no boost.

  1. ✎ Assign opens the pedalboard editor on the grid you play: tap a block, then a switch. Each lane has its own list of blocks; a switch can drive blocks of several lanes.

  1. Tuner turns the tuner on. It is one tuner for the whole rack: it shows over the Live page on every screen. It does not mute the sound.
  2. Click starts the metronome at the preset's tempo.
  3. Lock freezes the Designer during the set (and, on a rack, boosts the CPU): nobody can change the sound by mistake from another screen. Tap again to unlock.
  4. Rec appears when the mode allows recording: one tap records the take.

Next and previous song

The bar above the tiles is your place in the setlist.

  • In the middle, the line number and the song: SETLIST #09 · 9/9 Golem Rising, with the preset it uses (Ambient Metal) and its tempo in small letters. Tap it to see the songs of the setlist:

A tap on a song loads it — its sound, its tempo, its stems, its arrangement — exactly as the pedalboard would. With a bank of sounds on the foot, the list shows its sounds. Any other preset is played from Songs ▸ Presets.

  • ◀ and ▶ load the previous and the next line of the setlist, with their sound, their tempo and their arrangement. The names on each side tell you where they go — here One-Man Orchestra before, Rooftop Prowl after: the list wraps around.

  • The setlist button in the top bar (Factory Golem 9/19) changes setlist.

Your feet do the same: each bank of the pedalboard is one line of the setlist. UP and DOWN choose a bank on the pedalboard itself; the golem loads that line when you press a switch in it.
