The mixer

golem

The mixer balances what leaves the golem: each instrument, the backing tracks, the samples, the drum machine, the looper and the click. It is the last stage before your sound card — after your presets, after your scenes.

Output trims

Open it from the Live screen's toolbar (Mixer). There is one strip per source:

  • each instrument plugged into the card — here Guitar 1, on IN 1;
  • the players — Backing, Sample, Drums, Looper. A player the current preset does not have is greyed out, never removed: its level is kept for the next song that has it;
  • the click, always there.

A song with a Standard adds a strip per part it plays (piano, bass, drums…); a looper adds one per loop.

A trim only attenuates. The top of the fader is 0 — the sound as your preset makes it — and you pull it down from there, to −60 dB. There is no boost: gain added right before the converter is how you clip. MUTE silences the strip.

Trims belong to the golem, not to the song. Loading the next song never moves them: your balance stays the balance. They work with the rest of the rig, they replace nothing:

  • the scene level (each scene's level per instrument, in the preset) still says how loud a part is;
  • the volume pedal is still a playing control, inside your chain;
  • the mixer sets how loud each source leaves, all evening.

Linking strips. Each strip has a box under its fader, next to its level: tick two or more, drag one of their faders, and they all move together, keeping their gaps. Untick to free them; closing the mixer forgets the group.

REC MIX FOLLOWS: the recording of the processed sound follows the same trims. The DI recordings stay raw — the mixer never touches them.

Headphones and the PA

The header says where the mix goes: → OUT 1-2 · Monitors on the reference rack. On a card with one output pair — a Volt 276, a Scarlett 2i2 — the headphone jack plays the same thing as the main outputs: one mix, for the room and for you.

On a card with more outputs, each pair is declared in Settings ▸ Gear ▸ Soundcard. Today one trim applies to all of them: a separate headphone mix per musician is not built yet. A digital console (an XR18, an X32) mixes better than the golem and has its own app; how the golem hands its sources over to one is not decided yet.
