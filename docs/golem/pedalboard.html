The pedalboard and footswitches

golem

Your feet play the golem. On the reference rack the pedalboard is a Behringer FCB1010 with the UnO2 chip: ten footswitches, UP and DOWN, two expression pedals. The examples use Golem Rising from the Factory Golem setlist.

Scenes and stomps

The ten switches have two jobs.

  • Switches 1 to 5 are scenes A to E. A scene changes the whole band's sound at once. Pressing a scene switch also makes sure the right song is loaded first, then calls the scene.

  • Switches 6 to 10 are stomps: blocks you switch on and off, like pedals. Which block sits on which stomp is decided per preset. In Golem Rising, #6 is the Shimmer, #7 the Tempo Delay, #8 the singer's Grit, #9 starts the song's arrangement and #10 the drum machine.

  • The two expression pedals each have a role, Volume or Effect — here EXP 2 is the volume. The Effect pedal drives the knob of a block you assign to it (a wah, a whammy…).

Banks are songs. Each bank of the pedalboard is one line of the setlist on the foot (19 at most). UP and DOWN choose a bank on the pedalboard; the golem loads that song when you press a switch in it.

Calling a song by number: hold switch 1 for 1.5 seconds, then type two digits with the switches (1 to 9 are the digits 1 to 9, switch 10 is 0). The screen shows what you typed, with a Cancel button.

Assigning a switch

✎ Assign, in the toolbar of the Live screen, opens the pedalboard of the song that is loaded.

  • On the left, the blocks of each instrument, one tab per lane, plus Services (backing, recorder, tuner…). Tap a block, then tap a switch: it is assigned. ✕ on a line takes the block off the foot.
  • A switch can drive several blocks, even from several lanes. Each line of a switch says its lane — GTR, VOX, KEYS — and they all switch together.
  • ⇄ all moves everything on a switch to another one; each block keeps its settings and its scenes.
  • Scenes stay on 1 to 5. A service can take one of those switches for this preset — a recorder, a backing track — and the scene it replaces can still be called by the arrangement or from the screen.

The change is saved as you make it. Done closes the editor.

A block can also be put on a switch from its own drawer, on its MIDI assign tab — see Blocks.

Tap and long press

A footswitch understands more than a press — but only where it makes sense.

  • Stomps 6 to 9 are on/off switches with a lamp that stays lit. A tap switches; nothing else.
  • Some blocks own their switch and give it gestures — on switch 10, and on a scene switch a service takes, the switches that tell the golem both the press and the release. The Live tile shows them as • tap, •• double tap, ▬ hold, ▬▬ long hold: - Backing track: tap = play / pause; double tap = restart from the top; hold = stop and rewind. - Drums: tap = play / stop; hold = next variation, on the bar; long hold = stop. - Sample: tap = play the next sample; hold = stop. - Standard: tap = play / stop; hold = restart from the top. - Arrangement: tap = start / stop the song. - Looper: tap to record, tap to close the loop, double tap to overdub, hold to clear it.
  • Recording a take — the recorder and the backing on the same switch: one tap counts in, then starts both from the top; another tap stops both.

Gestures are the same on every golem: you learn them once. What you choose is which block sits on which switch.

Flashing the FCB1010 UnO2

The UnO2 chip keeps its own memory of what each switch sends. The golem writes it for you — that is flashing.

  • The pedalboard is recognised, not declared. The golem asks the chip who it is: ● MEASURED, its firmware and when it was last heard. A pedalboard that does not answer can be chosen by hand.

  • What each switch sends is on the page: scenes A–E on CC 20 to 24, stomps on CC 30 to 34, the two pedals on CC 7 and CC 11, all on MIDI channel 2.
  • The pedal roles — Volume or Effect — are set here, under each pedal. No flashing needed.

To flash, from Set up:

  1. Two MIDI cables: the card's MIDI OUT to the pedalboard's MIDI IN (to write the chip), and the pedalboard's MIDI OUT to the card's MIDI IN (to play). Switch the pedalboard on normally.
  2. Press ⚡ Compile & flash. Loading overwrites the pedalboard's memory: the screen says so.
  3. The chip confirms: ✓ The chip confirmed. The display reads 01.
  4. Check: switch 1's lamp stays lit, switch 6 latches on and off, switch 10 reacts on press and release, both pedals sweep.

On a Mac, the golem guides you through FCB UnO Control Center instead: it copies the setup, you send it from there.

When to flash again: after you change the setlist on the foot, or when a preset gives one of the switches 2 to 5 to an on/off block.

If it goes wrong, the screen says which case it is: no answer at all (check the power and both cables), an FCB without the UnO2 chip, or a write that started and never confirmed — then flash again before you play.
