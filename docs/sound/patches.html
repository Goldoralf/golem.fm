Patches

golem · golem.fm

A preset is a song. A patch is the sound of one musician in it: their lane, their scenes, and what they need to play it alone on the pedalboard. You keep patches like you keep pedal settings: build a sound once, then drop it into every song that needs it. Every golem ships with factory patches for guitar, bass, voice, keys and drums.

A patch is one chain

Songs ▸ Patches lists them. Each card is one sound:

  • its name and its instrument (Guitar, Bass, Voice, Keys, Drums), and the bank it sits on if it is on the pedalboard;
  • its chain, drawn small, from input to output;
  • its scenes A–E and stomps 6–10, on the right — tap them to see what changes;
  • its tags, to find it again. Filters at the top: instrument, musician, tag.

A patch never names a jack: it knows its role (a guitar patch looks for a guitar input) and plays wherever that instrument is plugged.

Dropping a patch into a lane

The factory presets are made of factory patches: each lane was built by dropping a patch into it.

  • The ⛓ chip at the top of a lane says which patch it came from — here Ambient Djent. Tap it to drop another patch into the lane instead.

  • The lane is replaced by the patch's chain, with its scenes. If the patch has a scene the song does not, the song gets it (up to eight scenes); the song's scene names stay.
  • The pedalboard follows: a block on switch 6 in the patch is on switch 6 in the song. If another lane already has a block on 6, switch 6 now drives both.
  • Only the sound travels. The patch's own players (looper, samples…) and its tags stay with the patch.

“edited”: when a lane is no longer its patch

When you change a lane after dropping a patch, the chip says so: ⛓ Modern Metal · edited. Metal for all's first guitar is the Modern Metal patch with one block added for this song: a volume pedal at the end of the chain.

What does not count as an edit: the golem renames blocks when two lanes would share a name, and gives the switches to the song's pedalboard. The chip only says edited when the sound changed — a block added or removed, a knob turned.

The song keeps its own copy: changing the lane never changes the patch, and changing the patch later does not change the songs by itself. To put the new version back into songs, use ⬇ Deposit in songs… on the patch: it replaces the lane of the songs you pick.

Trying a patch on the rack

▶ Play on a patch card loads it on the golem right away, at its first scene: you play it, and the Live screen and the pedalboard follow its scenes and stomps. ✎ Edit in the Designer opens it on its own — no song is affected while you work on it.

The trial lasts until you load something else. Your pedalboard does not move to another bank while you try a patch: the scene and stomp switches act on the patch, and pressing a switch of another bank loads that song — that is the way back.

Building a patch on golem.fm

On golem.fm, Library ▸ Patches ▸ + Add a patch builds a chain with a hand from the AI. golem.fm never edits a chain block by block the way the Designer does: the AI proposes, the golem's own Designer shows the result.

  1. Gear — what it's made of: name the patch, pick the instrument, and describe what you want: a band, a record, a song, or just a sound in your own words. You can add a pedal you want in the chain.
  2. Scenes — what you must play: the sounds the patch has to switch between, a clean and a distortion for instance.
  3. Wiring — how it's connected: the proposals answer scene by scene.
  4. Refine — and save: ask for changes in a sentence, then Save to the vault.

Each capture the chain needs says where it comes from: already in your golem.fm library, fetched by your golem from TONE3000, or built into the rack. ▶ Test on my golem sends the patch to your golem to play it now. Assisted designs are counted per month, depending on your plan.
