The drum lane

golem

A drummer gets a lane like anyone else: an input, a chain of blocks, scenes, a pedalboard. The golem plays drums in two ways — your electronic kit, played by a drummer, or the Drum Machine, played by the golem itself.

An electronic kit or the Drum Machine

An electronic kit comes in one of two ways:

  • through USB — the module's USB cable into one of the golem's USB ports. The golem receives what was played — every hit, its strength — and makes the sound with its own drum kits. It is also the only way a take keeps what the drummer played, not just the sound;
  • through the sound card — the module's left and right outputs into two inputs, linked as a stereo pair. The module makes the sound; the golem processes it like any other instrument.

Metal for all's drum lane is the first kind: USB in, then a Kit block (here drums-aasimonster), a compressor, an EQ and a room reverb on switch 6.

A drum lane's drawer only offers what suits drums — dynamics, EQ, a few modulations, delays, reverbs. No amp, no cabinet, no wah.

The Drum Machine is not a lane: it is a player of the preset, in the ⚙ lane next to the backing tracks, the sampler and the looper. It plays grooves (26 ship with golem, in Library ▸ Drums ▸ Grooves) on a rack kit, at the song's tempo, and starts and stops on its footswitch — or with the song, groove by groove, when the song has an arrangement.

Kits by song section

The Kit block's kit is a scene setting: each scene of the preset can play its own kit. A tight kit for the verse, a bigger one for the chorus — switch scenes and the drummer's sound follows, instantly. The golem prepares every kit the preset's scenes use when the preset loads, so a scene change only switches between them.

With an arrangement, the parts of the song choose the scenes (see The arrangement): the kit then changes by itself, part by part. The Drum Machine does the same with its grooves: each part of the song can pick its own.

Some rack kits are large sample libraries. A kit whose samples are not installed on this golem is marked — on the reference rack, Aasimonster: its samples are not installed on this machine — and the rest of the lane plays.

Calibrating a kit

Every module numbers its pads differently: a manual or a forum gives a lead, but only the pads are the truth. The golem measures your module once:

  1. Bind it on Settings ▸ Gear ▸ Soundcard: tap its USB port and choose Drums. The banner at the top says so when you plug a module in.
  2. Measure it on Settings ▸ Gear ▸ Drums: Measure asks for each element in turn — kick, snare, rim, each tom, the hi-hat closed, open, half-open and on its pedal, crashes and their choke, ride and bell. Hit it; the cell lights with the note and the strength. Skip what your kit does not have.
  3. Review and save. The golem compares what it measured with what it expected for that module, then saves your module as measured.

From then on, every drum patch and every factory preset plays your kit correctly: they ask for a kick, a snare, never for a note number.
