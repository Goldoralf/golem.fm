Blocks

golem

Everything you hear goes through blocks: an amp, a cabinet, a drive, a delay, a reverb, a piano, a drum kit. A lane is a chain of blocks; this page is about one block at a time. The examples open Ambient Metal, the preset of Golem Rising in the Factory Golem setlist.

The block catalogue

The golem has some seventy kinds of blocks, in families: dynamics (gate, compressor), drive, amp, cab, EQ, filter (wah…), modulation (chorus, phaser, tremolo…), delay, reverb, pitch, keys, drums, utility (volume, split…) and a few more. Each family has its colour and its icon, so a chain reads at a glance.

  • The catalogue follows the instrument. A voice lane is never offered an amp or a cabinet; a keys lane gets pianos and synths.
  • On the rack, ＋ between two blocks opens the catalogue: a family, then a block, then — for amps, pedals and cabinets — the capture you want.
  • Amps, pedals and cabinets are captures of real gear (NAM captures and impulse responses). A factory golem ships the links, not the files: until a capture is downloaded, its tile shows ⬇.

The banner at the top says how many blocks are missing and downloads them all in one tap — see Captures.

A block’s drawer: knobs, assign, presets

Tap a block and its drawer slides up. It has up to three tabs.

  • Settings — the block's knobs. Turning a knob changes it in the scene you are editing (the drawer says which). The switch at the top tells whether the block plays in this scene: ⏻ Active in B, or off in B. When a scene has its own knob values, Use in all scenes makes them the block's settings everywhere.

  • Presets — named settings for this block only, factory ones and yours. Loading one sets the block in every scene and clears its per-scene values; it never touches the rest of the song. Save the current settings as a preset of your own, for one instrument or all.
  • MIDI assign — which footswitch or expression pedal drives this block. Tap a switch to put the block on it, tap again to take it off. A long press on a tile opens the drawer straight on this tab.

Split, merge, A/B branches

A chain can split in two: two branches side by side, each with its own blocks.

  • Branch A and branch B. Golem Rising's guitar goes into a Peavey and a Mesa cabinet on A, a Roland on B. Each branch can be switched on or off per scene, with the A ⏻ and B ⏻ controls on the fork.

  • ⇉ or Σ. With ⇉ (fan-out) each branch goes to its own output: two amps to two speakers. With Σ (merge) the branches are summed back into one, with a level in dB (−3 dB here), and the chain goes on after it.

Two branches at most. On the rack, a split is made from a ＋ of the chain: ⑃ Split the chain. Removing a branch folds the chain back into one line.
