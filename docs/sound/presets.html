Presets and scenes

golem · golem.fm

A preset is the sound of the whole band for a song: every instrument, every effect, every scene, and what each footswitch does. You build it in the Designer; you play it on the Live screen and with your feet.

This page opens Ambient Metal, the preset of Golem Rising in the Factory Golem setlist. Load it on your golem (Songs ▸ Banks & Setlists ▸ Factory Golem, line 09, then the Designer) to follow along.

A preset is your rig

  • One tab per instrument — a lane. Ambient Metal has five: Guitar 1, Bass 1, Guitar 2, Voice 1 and a Piano. Under each name, the input it plays from (IN 1, USB 1; IN — when this sound card has no input left for it). The ⚙ tab holds what the whole band shares: the click, the recorder, the players, the arrangement.

  • The chain of the open lane, from the input on the left to the output on the right. Guitar 1 here: a gate, a compressor, a drive (switched off in this scene: its tile is dashed), then two amps side by side — a Peavey into a Mesa cabinet on branch A, a Roland on branch B — mixed back together, then EQ, chorus, delay and reverb. Tap a block to open its drawer.

  • The line above the chain tells where this preset lives. #09 Factory Golem: it is bank 9 of the setlist on your pedalboard (tap to move it). The small grid is your pedalboard in miniature — tap it to read what each switch does. The song loaded with the preset (its backing, its arrangement) and the tempo follow.

  • The patch chip says which patch this lane came from: Ambient Djent. When you change the lane, the chip adds · edited — the lane is no longer exactly that patch.

Scenes

A scene is a state of the whole preset — not of one instrument. When you step on a scene, every lane changes together: which blocks are on, how the knobs are set, how loud each instrument is.

  • The letters at the top are the scenes: A to E are the five scene switches of the pedalboard (#1 to #5). Tap a letter to edit that scene in the Designer; the chain shows what plays in it. Ambient Metal names them Halo Clean, Glass Verse, Low Riff, Wall Chorus and Soaring Solo.

  • The name of the scene you are editing sits on the line above the chain. Tap it to rename the scene and set the level of each instrument in it.

The scene sheet says it plainly: blocks on/off, knob overrides and the levels below are recalled together when you step on B. In Glass Verse, the second guitar sits 5 dB under the first.

A preset can have up to eight scenes; a new one is added from the first empty letter.

Scenes & sounds

Scenes & sounds (in the ⋯ menu) is the whole preset on one sheet: one row per instrument, one column per scene. Each cell is the sound that instrument plays in that scene, with a name you choose — Glass Arp, Sub Clean, Close Verse. A dotted cell (same as A) means the instrument does not change in that scene.

These names are not decoration. They are what the band reads on the Live screen, on their phones and in the arrangement: the singer sees Close Verse, not "compressor on, reverb 30 %". The row at the bottom shows exactly what the Live screen will show.

Saving

  • A preset saves itself. Every change is kept as you make it; the top bar of the Designer says ✓ saved. There is no save button to forget before the gig.
  • A new preset is not kept until it has a name: it shows ● not saved, and ⋯ ▸ Name & save (the ⋯ button at the top right) keeps it.
  • ⋯ ▸ ⧉ Duplicate makes a copy to try something without touching the original.

The factory presets are yours to change — and changing one changes it on your golem. Restore factory content (Settings ▸ Content ▸ Factory) brings back a factory preset you deleted, not one you edited. To keep Ambient Metal as it ships, duplicate it first and work on the copy.

Building a preset on golem.fm

You can also wire a preset from golem.fm, away from the rack: Library ▸ Presets ▸ + Add a preset.

  • Your patches are listed on the left. Drop one on an input of the row to make it a lane: a guitar patch on IN 1, a keys patch on a USB port. Name the preset and give it a tempo and a genre.
  • Card shows the inputs of your sound card. A lane on an input your card does not have stays in the preset: the rack mounts it where that input exists. A preset can address up to 18 audio inputs and 4 USB ports.
  • The screen beside the row is the golem's own Designer, showing your draft as the rack will.
  • Create preset, then ⤴ Push to rack on the preset's page: the rack installs it on its next pass. The preset page then shows its Live grid, and its arrangement once the rack has played it.
